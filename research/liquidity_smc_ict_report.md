# Liquidity concepts fail the Indian options test

"Liquidity" is not the key to trading. As ICT/SMC educators teach it (sweeps, stop hunts, equal highs/lows, order blocks, fair value gaps, killzones, Silver Bullet), it does not give a usable edge on Nifty, Bank Nifty or Sensex options. The idea has a real core: stop-loss and take-profit orders do cluster around obvious price levels, and that is documented by the New York Fed. But that research shows small effects, and it says breaks of those levels tend to *continue*, not reverse as the "sweep and reverse" story assumes. Every independent test that coded these rules mechanically found no edge over random price zones, or a thin edge that trading costs wiped out. Our own backtests on the FnO Trainer data are the most relevant evidence for this app, and they agree. We tested **more than 2,000 rule variants** across three indices and nearly four years, with full Indian F&O costs. The **average gross edge was roughly zero (−0.05R to +0.04R)**, **every ICT/SMC setup lost money on average**, and **none of 26 liquidity-based filters** reliably improved the existing Autopilot. Indian index options also suit the stop-hunt story poorly. The other side of a retail trade is usually a prop/algo market maker. Stop-loss *market* orders on index options have been banned since 2021. The one documented case of the index being deliberately pushed (SEBI's Jane Street order) was large, multi-segment manipulation, not stop-hunting. One positive lead came out of the work. "Displacement" (a strong momentum candle just before entry) marks the Autopilot trades that go on to earn more per trade, and is worth forward-testing as a **trade-size tilt**, not as a new strategy. That is a momentum finding, not proof that liquidity theory works.

## The vocabulary renames old ideas, and only part of it can be coded

Most of the ICT/SMC toolkit gives new names to older ideas. A "liquidity sweep" is the same footprint as Wyckoff's spring or upthrust and the Connors–Raschke Turtle Soup fade of false breakouts ([LuxAlgo: Spring](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/spring.md); [LuxAlgo: Turtle Soup](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/turtle-soup.md)). Order blocks overlap with supply/demand zones, which Sam Seiden popularised as "odds enhancers" in 2008 ([BabyPips forum: Seiden OTA lesson](https://forums.babypips.com/t/odd-enhancers-sam-seiden/39321)). Accumulation–manipulation–distribution (AMD, also called Power of 3) is a compressed Wyckoff sequence without Wyckoff's volume method ([LuxAlgo: AMD](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/accumulation-manipulation-distribution/)). The table below gives the commonly accepted definitions and says how codable each one is. That matters for an automated app, because a concept a computer cannot test is a concept nobody can verify.

| Concept | Plain-English meaning | Mechanical definition used by tools | How codable |
|---|---|---|---|
| Buy-side / sell-side liquidity (BSL/SSL), pools | Areas above highs / below lows where stop and breakout orders are *assumed* to rest | Swing highs/lows, equal highs/lows, previous-day high/low (PDH/PDL), session extremes, round numbers. These are inferred, not observed ([LuxAlgo](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/liquidity-pool/)) | Levels yes; the "orders behind them" no |
| Equal highs/lows (EQH/EQL) | Double tops/bottoms treated as stop magnets | Two or more swing extremes within a tolerance. No standard: the `smc` library uses 1% of the whole history's range ([smc.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/joshyattridge/smart-money-concepts/master/smartmoneyconcepts/smc.py)) | Depends on parameters |
| Sweep / stop hunt / Judas swing | Price pokes through a pool, then reverses | Trade through the level, then close back inside. Turtle Soup is the original 20-day version ([LuxAlgo](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/turtle-soup.md)) | Yes |
| BOS / CHoCH / MSS | Break of structure in the trend's direction / first break against it | Defined only once you fix swing length and whether a close or a wick counts. Sources disagree on MSS vs CHoCH ([LuxAlgo](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/change-of-character/)) | Depends on parameters; confirming swings needs lag to avoid lookahead |
| Displacement | A sharp, large-bodied move | "No fixed threshold" per LuxAlgo; a TradingView script uses range > k·σ ([LuxAlgo](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/displacement/)) | Depends on parameters |
| Fair value gap (FVG) | A 3-candle "imbalance" price is expected to revisit | Bullish when candle 1's high < candle 3's low ([LuxAlgo](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/fair-value-gap/)) | Yes, cleanly |
| Order block (OB) | The "last opposite candle" before a big move, said to hold institutional orders | Not standardised. The ICT "last opposing candle" and the `smc` library's "lowest-low candle" disagree ([LuxAlgo](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/order-block-anatomy-and-refinement.md); [smc.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/joshyattridge/smart-money-concepts/master/smartmoneyconcepts/smc.py)) | Conventions conflict |
| Breaker / mitigation block | A failed OB that flips role | Sources contradict each other on the definition ([LuxAlgo](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/mitigation-block/)) | Conventions conflict |
| Supply / demand zone | Base candles before a strong departure | Rally-base-rally, drop-base-rally, etc.; limit order at the proximal line ([LuxAlgo](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/supply-and-demand-zones/)) | Yes, with choices |
| Premium/discount, OTE | Buy below a range's 50%, sell above; enter at a 62–79% retracement | Fibonacci arithmetic on a chosen leg ([LuxAlgo: OTE](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/optimal-trade-entry/)) | Yes, once the leg is chosen |
| Killzones / Silver Bullet | Time windows said to be when "smart money" acts | New York clock hours; no audited statistics exist ([LuxAlgo](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/silver-bullet/)) | Yes (time filter) |
| CISD | Body close beyond the open of the last run of opposite candles | Precise rule ([LuxAlgo](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/change-in-state-of-delivery.md)) | Yes |
| SMT divergence | One correlated index makes a new high and the other does not | Compare swing extremes across a pair ([LuxAlgo](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/smart-money-technique-divergence/)) | Yes |
| IPDA, daily bias, "draw on liquidity", Market Maker Models | The claim that an interbank algorithm "delivers" price between pools | Even sympathetic toolmakers call IPDA "not something verifiable from public data" ([LuxAlgo: IPDA](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/ipda-and-price-delivery-theory/)) | No: discretionary and unfalsifiable |

So FVG, the sweep-and-reclaim, CISD, OTE, premium/discount, session levels and time windows are objective. Order blocks, breakers, displacement, equal highs and swing structure depend on parameters, and each choice changes the results. The grand narrative (IPDA, bias, "smart money intends to…") cannot be tested at all. In Indian adaptations, the killzones map onto 09:15–10:15 and 14:30–15:30 IST, and the opening range and PDH/PDL take the role of the Asian/London range. Those mappings come from broker blogs such as [Sahi](https://sahi.com/blogs/liquidity-sweep-scalping-strategy-guide) and TradingView authors, not from ICT, and no backtest accompanies them.

## Stops do cluster, but breaks continue more often than they reverse

The academic support for "liquidity" is real but narrow. Carol Osler at the New York Fed studied the Royal Bank of Scotland's full stop-loss and take-profit book: **9,655 orders worth over $55bn**. She found stop-loss buys cluster just *above* round numbers (14.3% of executed stop buys in the 01–10 range vs 6.9% in 90–99) and stop-loss sells just below ([Osler, FRBNY SR150](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/staff_reports/sr150.pdf)). Two behaviours follow, and they point in opposite directions. Take-profit orders at round numbers produce a small *reversal* effect (59.3% vs 54.8% at arbitrary levels) that is significant only for about 30 minutes. Stop-loss orders produce faster *continuation* after a level is crossed, and that effect is still visible two hours later ([Osler SR150](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/staff_reports/sr150.pdf)). A companion study found published support/resistance levels "bounced" **60.8% of the time vs 56.2% for random levels**, an edge of about 4.6 percentage points, and it was weaker in volatile periods ([Osler 2000, FRBNY EPR](https://resources.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/epr/00v06n2/0007osle.pdf)). Peer-reviewed work also shows that support/resistance lines up with peaks in limit-order-book depth ([Kavajecz & Odders-White, RFS 2004](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/rfinst/v17y2004i4p1043-1071.html)).

This evidence supports "orders gather at obvious levels." It does not support "banks deliberately hunt your stops." Osler records stop-running as market lore and explicitly does not test it. Her sources reported large cascades about once a week, against round-number crossings about five times a day ([Osler SR150](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/staff_reports/sr150.pdf)). The size of these effects, a few percentage points of hit rate, is also nowhere near the 70–80% win rates educators advertise. The data are FX from 1996–2000, before algorithmic trading.

The intraday effects with stronger evidence are all momentum: break and continue. Market intraday momentum, where the first half-hour predicts the last half-hour, is published in the Journal of Financial Economics ([Alpha Architect summary of Gao, Han, Li & Zhou](https://alphaarchitect.com/2014/08/attention-prop-traders-the-first-half-hour-of-trading-predicts-the-last-half-hour/)). Opening-range breakouts on high-attention US "stocks in play" reportedly show a Sharpe of 2.81 net of costs ([Concretum Group](https://concretumgroup.com/a-profitable-day-trading-strategy-for-the-u-s-equity-market/)). Time-series momentum holds across 58 futures markets ([AQR](https://www.aqr.com/library/journal-articles/time-series-momentum)). A sweep-reversal of the opening range is the mirror image of the best-documented opening-range effect, so it bets against the evidence. The one Indian paper on short-horizon behaviour found returns reversing within minutes after extreme moves, but it did not include costs, and its details sit behind a paywall ([Tripathi & Aggarwal 2015](https://publishingindia.com/archive/ijfm/prior-return-effect-in-indian-stock-market-an-intra-day-analysis)). No study was found of PDH/PDL or round-number reversals on Nifty or Bank Nifty.

## Independent tests find FVGs and order blocks behave like random zones

The claims built on social media do not survive a fair test, for one consistent reason: high "fill" and "hold" rates are the base rate for any zone near price. An editorial test on ES futures found FVGs were revisited in the same session **82% of the time, and random zones of the same size and distance also 82%**. Order blocks held 82% vs 78% for random zones on ES, and 47% vs 51% on EUR/USD ([AlgoStorm](https://algostorm.com/ict-smc-key-concepts/)). The samples were small and no costs were included, but the design was the right one. Fully specified tests that included costs did no better. A 10-year NAS100 FVG strategy returned **profit factor 1.08 and 3.3% CAGR against 527% for buy-and-hold**. A GBP/USD killzone-FVG strategy returned **profit factor 0.80 and lost money in every calendar year** ([Backtrex NAS100](https://backtrex.com/en/backtests/ict-fair-value-gap-nasdaq); [Backtrex GBP/USD](https://backtrex.com/en/backtests/ict-kill-zone-fvg-gbp-usd)). A broker's internal test reported that hand-reviewed SMC setups showed 70%+ win rates while coded rules showed about 41%, and that standalone order blocks failed 56.9% of the time. Its figures do not reconcile and the broker has a commercial interest ([FXNX](https://fxnx.com/en/blog/smart-money-concepts-work-backtest-evidence)). That gap between hand-picked and coded results is exactly what hindsight chart-marking would produce. One SSRN working paper on G10 FX is titled "Smart Money or Costly Folklore?" Its contents could not be retrieved, so its findings are unverified ([SSRN 7430998](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=7430998)). No peer-reviewed test of these setups was found, and no independent test on Indian indices.

The educators' own records add nothing in favour. ICT's founder, Michael Huddleston, has no audited long-term live record. His public 2016 $10k-to-$1M challenge and his 2024 Robbins Cup account reportedly ended in heavy drawdowns; that comes from a secondary source, and the Robbins leaderboard was not checked ([Complete Trader's Edge](https://completetradersedge.com/michael-j-huddleston-ict-inner-circle-trader/)). Indian SMC content mostly applies ICT vocabulary to Bank Nifty with "trade like institutions/FIIs" framing and no performance data. Examples are an Udemy "BankNIFTY FVG & Liquidity Mastery" course ([Udemy](https://www.udemy.com/course/liquidity-fvg-mastery-for-banknifty/)) and Booming Bulls' SMC video, which links Telegram and broker sign-ups ([VideoHighlight](https://videohighlight.com/v/hXtpVnpjuXg)). The verifiable records that do exist in India come from SEBI enforcement. SEBI found "Baap of Chart" had concealed ₹2.89 crore of trading losses while claiming 20–30% profits ([Moneylife](https://moneylife.in/article/sebi-bans-finfluencer-nasir-ansari-of-baap-of-charts-6-others-from-markets-asks-them-to-refund-rs172-crore-to-investors/75773.html)). It also found that Avadhut Sathe's academy promotions showed only profitable trades, and ordered ₹546 crore disgorged ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/markets/news/avadhut-sathe-finfluencer-sebi-ban-disgorgement-unregistered-advisory-125120500178_1.html)). Since January 2025, SEBI has limited educators to price data at least three months old and banned unapproved performance claims ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/markets/news/sebi-finfluencer-circular-live-stock-data-market-education-rules-125013000571_1.html)). The business model explains the pattern: course fees, Telegram tiers, broker referrals and prop-firm affiliate fees of $40–60 per challenge sale pay out whether or not students profit ([Track360](https://track360.io/learn/scaling-prop-trading-affiliates/influencer-community-recruitment)). The base rate is stark. SEBI found **93% of more than 1 crore individual F&O traders lost money in FY22–FY24** ([SEBI](https://www.sebi.gov.in/media-and-notifications/press-releases/sep-2024/updated-sebi-study-reveals-93-of-individual-traders-incurred-losses-in-equity-fando-between-fy22-and-fy24-aggregate-losses-exceed-1-8-lakh-crores-over-three-years_86906.html)).

## Indian index options are a poor fit for the stop-hunt story

The SMC picture of a big player sweeping retail stops does not match how Nifty and Bank Nifty options actually trade. In FY27 to date, **prop firms are 48.3% of equity options premium turnover and individuals 39.2%**; FPIs and other institutions are single digits ([NSE Market Pulse Sep 2026](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com//web/mediaattachment/2026-09/Market_Pulse_September_2026_Final.pdf)). In FY24, 96% of prop profits came from algorithmic trading ([Outlook Money](https://outlookmoney.com/invest/equity/90-individuals-incur-loss-in-fo-trading-proprietary-traders-fpis-make-huge-profit-how-they-do-it)). The usual counterparty is therefore a market-making algorithm earning spread and selling volatility. The plumbing also differs from FX or crypto. NSE discontinued **stop-loss market orders for index options from 27 September 2021** ([Zerodha](https://zerodha.com/marketintel/bulletin/305785/stop-loss-market-sl-m-orders-blocked-for-index-options)), and BSE followed across segments in October 2023 ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/markets/news/bse-to-discontinue-stop-loss-market-orders-from-oct-9-to-check-freak-trades-123092200889_1.html)). Retail option stops are now stop-limit orders or broker-held GTTs, which are invisible in the exchange book and can skip in a fast move. An option's own "prior low" mostly reflects index movement plus time decay and IV, not a cluster of stops in that contract.

The case that *is* documented points the other way. SEBI's July 2025 interim order alleged that Jane Street bought Bank Nifty stocks and futures in the morning while building a much larger short-delta options book, then dumped them in the afternoon. On 17 January 2024 that meant ₹4,370 cr of cash/futures buying against ₹32,115 cr of options sales, for a net ₹673 cr gain ([Moneycontrol via TradingView](https://tr.tradingview.com/news/moneycontrol%3A0e6320645094b%3A0-how-jane-street-allegedly-manipulated-index-closing-on-expiry-days)). The order is under appeal. The path it describes, a morning push and an afternoon reversal, looks like a "Judas swing." But the mechanism was moving the index to reprice a huge options position. Retail cannot see it in real time, and it is not stop liquidity. SEBI has since rebuilt the regime to suppress that behaviour. It brought in one weekly index expiry per exchange (Bank Nifty weeklies ended 13 November 2024) ([Angel One](https://www.angelone.in/news/market-updates/nse-ends-weekly-expiry-for-key-index-derivatives)), delta-based and intraday position limits ([Value Research](https://www.valueresearchonline.com/stories/224848/sebi-rewrites-fo-rules-to-curb-expiry-day-speculation/)), Tuesday/Thursday expiries ([Moneylife](https://moneylife.in/article/nse-equity-fo-contracts-to-expire-on-tuesdays-bse-on-thursdays-from-1st-september/77434.html)), and from April 2026 higher STT of **0.15% on option premium** ([5paisa](https://www.5paisa.com/news/stt-hike-on-fo-to-take-effect-from-april-1-amid-rising-options-activity)). The only Indian study of expiry pinning found **no max-pain effect** ([IIMB](https://repository.iimb.ac.in/handle/2074/21032?mode=full)). Many Bank Nifty sweep setups taught online were calibrated on pre-November-2024 weekly-expiry behaviour that no longer exists. Higher costs hit hardest on the tight-stop, high-R:R entries that SMC favours.

## Our own backtests: 2,000+ variants with no gross edge

These are the tests that matter most for the FnO Trainer, because they use its own data, its own option P&L model and its own Autopilot rules. They were run on the research-data branch of the FnO repo (scripts `liquidity.py`, `amd_poc.py`, `pcp_arb.py`, `round47_smc.py`, `round46.py`, `round48`/`round48b`).

The data were Nifty, Bank Nifty and Sensex 5-minute candles from 3 January 2023 to 9 October 2026 (about 929 sessions each), plus ten large-cap stocks with real volume and recorded option candles from May–October 2026. Trades were priced as **1-ITM index options**, using Black-Scholes premiums from India VIX calibrated to real option prices, with **0.5% slippage per fill, full Indian F&O charges, and one lot**. The gross-edge benchmark compares each trade with a random-direction "twin" on the same bar with the same stop and R:R. For limit-order entries, where the random twin is biased negative, the comparison is with 0R instead. The early period (2023–24) and late period (2025–26) give an out-of-sample check: pick the best variant on the early period, then report its late-period result.

Test 1 contains one useful detail. Pools built from PDH/PDL and the opening range had the best gross result (+0.13R vs +0.035R for the random twin), and equal highs/lows had the worst (−0.16R). That fits Osler: obvious, widely watched levels matter somewhat, and the EQH/EQL "stop magnet" story has no support. The best variant (Bank Nifty key pools + CHoCH, 3R target) made +₹20k on 22 trades in four years, almost all in 2023. That is too few trades, concentrated in one year, to mean anything.

Test 4 is the most complete test of the toolkit. It uses a strict fill rule: a limit entry that also hits its stop within the same bar counts as a full loss. An earlier run that dropped such trades overstated the order-block and FVG results, which shows how easily these setups flatter a backtest. The filters behaved in a revealing way. The 15-minute trend filter helped: average net improved from −₹1.42L to −₹0.61L, but mostly because it cut the number of trades. The two most "ICT-specific" filters, premium/discount and killzones, *lowered* average R. Targeting the next liquidity pool was the worst target choice. The out-of-sample test settles the question. For each setup and index, the best 2023–24 variant **stayed positive in 2025–26 in only 7 of 39 cases**, and the 39 together lost ₹11.5L in the late period. The best result in the whole grid was a Nifty breaker block with the 15-minute trend filter and a 3R target: +₹95k on 381 trades, positive every year, average +0.14R ± 0.07R. That is about two standard errors, found among 1,248 tries. Sullivan, Timmermann and White's data-snooping work shows that at this level of searching, such a result is what chance alone produces ([LSE Research Online](https://researchonline.lse.ac.uk/id/eprint/119144)). It goes on the watch list, not into Autopilot.

### As add-ons to Autopilot, liquidity concepts do not pass

Test 5 asked a more practical question: even if these concepts fail on their own, do they improve the Autopilot rules that already make money? The baseline is 2,150 trades on the three indices from January 2023 to October 2026, earning **+₹4.76L per lot in 2023–25 and +₹1.28L in 2026**. Compounding a ₹2L account turns it into **₹29.8L, with a worst fall of −30.3%**. A filter passed only if it improved *both* periods *and* beat a shuffle test at p < 0.05.

**None of the 26 filters passed.** The list covered sweeps for and against the trade, a pool ahead, HH/HL agreement, BOS, CHoCH against the trade, inside a box, box breakout, manipulation, today's and yesterday's POC, FVG inside or opposite FVG ahead, OB, supply/demand ahead, premium/discount, killzones, SMT, EQH/EQL ahead, and displacement as a filter. Four near-misses improved both periods but only at p ≈ 0.15–0.20. They were: skip if an opposite open FVG sits within 1 ATR ahead (account ₹31.5L), skip if equal highs/lows sit within 1 ATR ahead, skip inside a tight 30-minute box, and skip after a 5-minute CHoCH against the trade (₹31.4L, but worse in 2023–25). Taking profit at the next liquidity pool *reduced* per-lot profit, to ₹3.52L vs ₹4.76L and ₹1.11L vs ₹1.28L. Both liquidity add-on setups dragged the compounded account down: PDH/PDL sweep + CHoCH to ₹22.1L, and the 60-minute box breakout in trend to ₹20.4L.

## Displacement is the one lead, and it is momentum, not liquidity

Test 6 used displacement differently. It defined it as **a candle with a body of at least 1 ATR in the trade's direction among the last three 5-minute bars before entry**, and used it as a marker of trade quality rather than as an entry or a skip rule. Autopilot trades with displacement earned more per trade in three of four years, and the effect was strongest in 2026, the most recent and least-mined year.

| Period | Per trade with displacement | Per trade without |
|---|---|---|
| 2023 (the exception) | ₹40 | ₹79 |
| 2024 | ₹700 | ₹196 |
| 2025 | ₹379 | ₹227 |
| 2026 | ₹962 (137 trades) | −₹15 (296 trades) |

The result held on every index (Nifty ₹649 vs ₹175, Sensex ₹509 vs ₹60, Bank Nifty ₹224 vs ₹100) and on every Autopilot rule family (Camarilla, Fibonacci, Noise band, Trend-day). A block bootstrap puts the difference at **+₹386 per trade (90% range ₹116–₹666), with P(≤0) = 0.007**. That is strong but not conclusive. About 12 features were tested in that round, so a Bonferroni-corrected threshold would be about 0.004, and the result sits just outside it.

Displacement is not useful as a filter. Keeping only displacement trades improved 2026 (₹1.60L vs ₹1.28L) but cut 2023–25 to ₹3.64L. Skipping displacement trades wiped out 2026 (−₹4k). It works as a sizing tilt:

| Sizing rule (₹2L account) | Full history (worst fall) | 2026 alone | Bootstrap 1-yr median | Chance of 30%+ fall | Chance of a losing year |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Baseline | ₹29.8L (−30.3%) | ₹4.63L | ₹3.53L | 26.9% | 11.3% |
| 1.5× with displacement, 0.75× without | ₹62.6L (−29.2%) | ₹6.34L | ₹4.05L | 30.1% | 10.7% |
| 1.5× with displacement only | ₹72.4L (−30.4%) | — | — | 36.5% | — |

The balanced 1.5×/0.75× tilt is the sensible version. It raises the typical one-year outcome by about 15% (₹3.53L to ₹4.05L median) and leaves the losing-year chance roughly flat. It also raises the chance of a 30%+ drawdown by about three points, so part of the gain is added risk, not free edge. The large full-history multiple (₹62.6L vs ₹29.8L) mostly reflects compounding over four years and should not be read as the expected improvement. The 1.5×-only version adds even more drawdown risk (36.5%) for the extra return and should not be used.

The interpretation matters for the user's broader question. A big candle in the trade's direction just before entry is a **momentum-continuation** signal. That matches Osler's continuation-after-breaks finding and the intraday-momentum and breakout literature. It contradicts the sweep-and-reverse logic at the centre of ICT/SMC. The one piece of ICT vocabulary that helped is the one that measures trend strength, not the ones that claim to read hidden orders.

## Verdict and next steps for the FnO Trainer

The verdict is clear. Liquidity/SMC/ICT concepts are not the key to trading Indian index options, and the FnO Trainer should not adopt them as entries, exits or filters. Three independent lines of evidence agree. First, the academic literature finds that order clustering is real but small, and that breaks tend to continue. Second, independent backtests find FVGs and order blocks perform like random zones. Third, our own 2,000+ variant tests, with full Indian costs, found zero average gross edge, consistent net losses, and out-of-sample decay of the best variants. The concepts work as a *vocabulary* for describing charts after the fact. They do not produce a testable, repeatable advantage once coded.

The practical next steps follow from the data. Ship displacement as a **switchable sizing tilt** in paper mode: 1.5× size when a ≥1 ATR body in the trade's direction appears in the last three 5-minute bars, 0.75× otherwise. Every paper trade should be tagged with the flag so both groups keep accumulating regardless of the switch. Set the pass test before looking at results, so the forward test cannot be fitted afterwards. As a suggested standard, require at least about 100 forward displacement trades, roughly eight to nine months at the 2026 rate of 137 trades in about nine months. The tilt passes only if displacement trades keep a clearly higher per-trade average than non-displacement trades on at least two of the three indices. Until then, keep live sizing at baseline. Put four items on a passive watch list, logged as tags with no effect on trading: the opposite-FVG-within-1-ATR skip, the EQH/EQL-within-1-ATR skip, the tight-30-minute-box skip, and the Nifty breaker + 15-minute trend + 3R setup. Re-check them only if forward data pushes them past the original pass rule (both periods better, shuffle p < 0.05). Drop the sweep-reversal and PDH/PDL-sweep add-ons, liquidity-pool profit targets, premium/discount and killzone filters, the POC/"manipulation" reel strategy, and put-call parity arbitrage. The tests showed these either reduce Autopilot's returns or vanish once realistic fills are modelled. When liquidity levels are useful at all, treat PDH/PDL, the opening range and round numbers as places where breaks are more likely to *continue*, not as reversal triggers.

## Conclusion

The research changes the question. "Does liquidity work?" assumes the toolkit measures something hidden, the footprints of institutions hunting stops. In Indian index options that hidden thing is mostly absent. Stop-market orders are banned, the counterparty is a market-making algorithm, and the one proven case of index-pushing ran through thousands of crores of cash and futures trades. What the toolkit actually measures, once coded, is ordinary price geometry, and that geometry performs like random zones. The single useful signal, displacement, is valuable for the reason momentum research predicts, not the reason liquidity theory gives. That gives the user a filter for the next viral "smart money" reel: ask whether the claim can be written as a rule, whether it beats a random-level twin after Indian costs, and whether it survives a period it was not tuned on. Almost nothing in this toolkit passes all three.

A caveat applies to our own evidence. Our option P&L comes from VIX-calibrated Black-Scholes pricing, not from tick-level fills across the full history. Real option candles exist only from May 2026, and the 2023–24 Bank Nifty data comes from the old weekly-expiry era. These limits cut both ways, but they are not large enough to turn a zero gross edge into a real one. The displacement tilt is the only finding that needs live forward data before anyone trusts it.
